- `i_see_you.wav`: generated locally with Windows' installed Microsoft David speech voice, speaking "I see you." at rate -3. Playback pitch is 0.76. No external audio download or package is required.
- `chase_music.wav`: original eight-second mono 22,050 Hz PCM loop, generated by `generate_horror_audio.gd`; low detuned drone, accelerating pulse, and dissonant alarm tones.
- `floor_tap.wav`: original short mono PCM sole impact with a hard initial tap and low thump, generated by the same script. Played in 3D at Ronald's feet, with slight pitch variation and distance attenuation.
- `catch_scream.wav`: original 1.15-second synthesized vocal shriek, using a rising, rough harmonic voice with vowel formants and breath noise; generated by the same script. Plays once per catch at -5 dB.
- `screen_static.wav`: original half-second filtered noise loop from the same generator. Plays at -17 dB during face overlays, then stops. Pause and screen cleanup stop it immediately.
- `bite_crunch.wav`: original 0.55-second series of filtered-noise/low-thump bursts, generated by the same script. Plays during the grapple-failure bite cutscene at -6 dB.

Regenerate music and tapping with `godot --headless --path . -s systems/hiding/generate_horror_audio.gd`, then import the project. Voice regeneration uses Windows `System.Speech.Synthesis.SpeechSynthesizer` with `SetOutputToWaveFile`, `Rate = -3`, and `Speak('I see you.')`.

Presentation lives in `horror_feedback.gd`. It reads the existing hiding leak and the current legacy actor's chase state without changing the shared blackboard. Audio starts during play. Voice fires once per leak, music starts once per chase and stops on loss of detection or game end; pause uses Godot's pausable processing. The graphical preview script exercises playback; listening and volume/feel approval remain human checks.

`horror_events.gd` owns scream/static playback. The separate 512-pixel portrait is used only for occasional "you better run" warnings after Ronald spawns. Catch no longer uses that GUI: `catch_cutscene.gd` moves the actual live actor through a jump arc toward a 3D camera, while the scream plays once. The normal death screen follows the leap/close-up. `preview_spawned_cutscenes.gd` verifies the normal first-fuse spawn, timeout sequence and AI catch with real-time processing. Preview screenshots are kept under ignored `reviews/`.
